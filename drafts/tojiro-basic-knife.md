# Tojiro Basic gyuto (draft notes)

Suggested slug: `tojiro-basic-knife`
Suggested title angle: "Why I paid $75 for a knife when the internet said $50"

## 1. Trigger

- Started cooking, wanted one good-quality knife instead of whatever was in the drawer.
- Started talking to friends who cook and doing research online.

## 2. Requirements

- Original budget: ~$50.
- One knife that does everything (a chef's knife / gyuto). [VERIFY: was it 200 or 210mm? Basic gyuto is 200mm]
- Not stated in notes but implied: good steel, comfortable handle, real value for money.

## 3. Shortlist and why each lost

- **The two Western chef's knives every list recommends at $50**
  - [FILL IN: which two? Almost certainly Victorinox Fibrox Pro 8" and Mercer Culinary Genesis / Renaissance. Confirm.]
  - Several reviews said best value for money. Was about to buy one.
  - Why they lost: people I talked to said spending a bit more is better, but only up to the point where diminishing returns kick in. This is the key insight of the post.
- **Korin's house brand (Togiharu)** [VERIFY: "koji? store" in notes. Korin in NYC is almost certainly the store. Togiharu is their house brand.]
  - Went to the store and tested their cheaper knives in person.
  - Research confirms your read: Togiharu is made by a major Japanese maker (widely reported to be Tojiro) and Korin charges more for one-by-one inspection and hand-finished edges.
  - Why it lost: paying extra for QA on what is essentially the same knife.
- **Tojiro DP (the "regular" line)**
  - Same VG-10 core, 3-layer construction. Nicer finishing, better handle fit, higher price (roughly 2x the Basic for a gyuto).
  - Why it lost: "no real benefit with the regular" for a first knife. Performance is the same steel.

## 4. The pick

- Tojiro Basic gyuto, $75 shipped. [VERIFY: current listings ~$72-75, so this checks out]
- Went with the Basic over the DP because the steel is identical and the handle is fine.

## 5. After real use

- Use it a lot. Very good purchase.
- Handle is nicer than expected for the price, "shows it's a good knife".
- Harder steel, so it holds an edge longer than the $50 German-style knives. Trade-off: it will be harder to sharpen later and chips easier if abused. [FILL IN: have you sharpened it yet? On what?]
- Other reviews call it the best-value Japanese knife. My take: it is a best-value knife, period.
